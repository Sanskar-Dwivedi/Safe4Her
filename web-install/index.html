Step 1 of 6

Install in six steps

About five minutes

  1. Download the watch app to your computer. You also need Android platform tools, which gives you the adb command. Put the watch app in the platform tools folder to keep things simple.

    Download watch app

  2. On the watch, open Settings, then System, then About. Tap Build number seven times until it says you're a developer. On some watches it's under About, then Versions.

  3. Go back to Settings and open Developer options. Turn on ADB debugging and Debug over Wi-Fi, then open Wireless debugging. Keep the watch and computer on the same Wi-Fi.

  4. Tap Pair new device on the watch and type what it shows. Then run the command and enter the six-digit code.

  5. Back on the Wireless debugging screen, the watch shows its address with a different port. Type that port, then run both commands from the folder with the watch app.

    adb install Security4Her-watch.apk

  6. Open the phone app first, then Security4Her on the watch. They find each other on their own, and the phone shows Watch linked. You can turn wireless debugging off again.

Your watch is ready.

Try it with Test Mode on in the phone app: press and hold SOS on the watch and see the alert arrive on your phone.

Stuck somewhere?

Most install problems are one of these. The install steps remember where you were, so you can pick up where you left off.

Phone

Chrome says the file might be harmful

Chrome shows that warning for every app that doesn't come from the Play Store. Tap Download anyway. To check it's the real file, compare its SHA-256 fingerprint with the one at the bottom of this page.

Nothing happens when I tap the file

Android needs permission for Chrome (or your Files app) to install apps. Open Settings, search for "Install unknown apps", pick the app you're opening the file from and turn on Allow from this source.

It says "App not installed"

An older copy of Security4Her is probably still on the phone. Uninstall it, then open the file again. Also check you have about 80 MB free.

It says Android blocked SMS for this app

Android 13 and newer does this for every app installed from a download. Open Settings, then Apps, then Security4Her. Tap ⋮ at the top right, choose Allow restricted settings and confirm, then go to Permissions, SMS and choose Allow. Until you do, an SOS still works: it opens Messages with your text and contacts filled in, so it's one tap to send. Installing from a computer with adb install avoids the block completely.

Why does it ask for so many permissions?

Location to share where you are, SMS and phone to reach your contacts without internet, microphone and camera to record evidence during an SOS, and notifications to show the alert. Nothing is recorded or shared unless an SOS or Safe Journey is running.

Watch

adb says "command not found"

Run the commands from inside the platform tools folder. On Windows use .\adb in PowerShell; on Mac or Linux use ./adb.

Pairing fails or times out

The pairing code and port change every time you open Pair new device, so type the ones showing right now. The watch and computer must be on the same Wi-Fi, and some guest or office networks block this.

The phone never shows Watch linked

The watch must be paired with the phone in the Wear OS or Galaxy Wearable app first. Open the Security4Her phone app, then the watch app, and give it a few seconds.

Trying it out

Can I try an SOS without alerting anyone?

Yes. Turn on Test Mode in Settings. Texts and the automatic call go to a demo number you choose, and Safe Journey timers are shortened so you can see an alert in a minute.
