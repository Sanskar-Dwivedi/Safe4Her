Help us test Security4Her

We're testing Security4Her with students before it launches for everyone. Use it like you normally would. If something breaks, the app can tell us what went wrong, but only if you say yes.

Install the betaRead the privacy notice

How it works

  1. Install the phone appDownload it from the install page. The watch app installs from the phone, from Me → Install or update the watch app.
  2. Choose what to shareThe first time you open it, the app asks which test data you're happy to share. Pick any, all or none. The app works exactly the same either way.
  3. Use it as normalTry an SOS in Test Mode, start a Safe Journey, test Fake call and the Siren. Use the watch if you have one.
  4. Tell us when something's offWith sharing on, open Me → Beta testing → Report a problem. Say what happened and you can attach the app's recent errors.

What you can share

Each kind is a separate choice, and you can change them whenever you like.

Crash reports

When the app closes unexpectedly: what went wrong in the code.

Errors

A text that didn't send, GPS that didn't answer, an update that didn't install.

Security events

A wrong safety PIN, or evidence or a download that fails its check.

False alarms

A pocket or watch countdown you cancelled, so we can make triggers smarter.

Feature use

Which features you use: SOS, Safe Journey, Fake call, Siren.

Speed

How long the app takes to start and how fast an SOS goes out.

Phone model

Brand, model and Android version, to find phone-specific bugs.

Your reports

Problems you send from Me → Beta testing → Report a problem.

Never collected

Not shared, ever
  • Your location
  • Phone numbers and contact names
  • Messages and recordings
  • Your name, account or Aadhaar details
How it's kept safe
  • You're a random tester ID, not a person
  • Numbers, places, links and names are removed on your phone first
  • Sent encrypted to a private database only the team can read
  • Deleted when the beta ends

You're in control

Change your choices

Phone: Me → Beta testing → Change what I share. Watch: Beta test data on the home screen.

Delete everything you sent

Phone: Me → Beta testing → Stop sharing and delete my test data. On the watch, turning sharing off deletes it.

What's new

1.1.0-beta.11 (2026-10-02)

Fixed

  • The phone's own Emergency SOS no longer starts with Pocket SOS. Android's built-in SOS (and the double-press camera) only counts power presses that come within about half a second of each other. Pocket SOS now asks for a calm rhythm, about one press a second, and accepts presses up to 2 s apart. Tested on Android 15: three presses a second apart arm Pocket SOS every time and the system counts no fast taps at all; five fast presses still start the system SOS, as before.

1.1.0-beta.10 (2026-10-02)

Fixed

  • Pressing power 3 times no longer runs into the phone's own Emergency SOS. Pocket SOS used to wait 0.8 s after the third press to see if more were coming, so people kept pressing and reached the 5 presses that start Android's built-in Emergency SOS. It now arms on the third press with a long buzz, and any extra presses count as the same alarm. Me → Pocket SOS links straight to the phone's own Emergency SOS switch (tested on Android 15).
  • Three presses were sometimes missed: Android can deliver the screen on/off events bunched together (70 ms apart for presses half a second apart), and those were taken for duplicates.
  • After cancelling a false alarm, Pocket SOS ignored the power button for 10 seconds. It's ready again at once.

1.1.0-beta.9 (2026-10-02)

Changed

  • Pocket SOS: press the power button 3 times (about half a second apart, screen on or off). It replaces knocking as the default; knocking stays available as an option. It uses no motion sensor and no always-on wake lock, so it works on phones that pause sensors with the screen off and uses less battery. Presses during a phone call are ignored (the screen flips against her ear), and the test in Me → Pocket SOS reports how many presses were noticed.

1.1.0-beta.8 (2026-10-02)

Security

  • Evidence opens only in Security4Her. The copies in Downloads are now encrypted .s4he packages (AES-256-GCM, a fresh key for every hour of every SOS, protected by a 256-bit recovery secret kept in the Android Keystore). Galleries, file managers and other apps can't play them; the readable fingerprints.txt is gone. Copies saved by beta.7 are encrypted and the plain ones deleted automatically.
  • Recovery code and restore. The evidence locker shows a recovery code (PIN-protected) and restores evidence from Downloads after a reinstall, offline. Tested: record, uninstall, reinstall, restore, all pieces intact.
  • SOS texts can't trip Android's SMS limit. Android holds an app's texts behind an "allow?" prompt after about 30 in 30 minutes. First alerts always go; repeat location updates are one SMS long and pause before the limit, while the live link keeps updating.

Changed

  • The police number is 100 (call buttons, the SOS screen and the default police text number).

Removed

  • The Siren.

1.1.0-beta.7 (2026-10-02)

Security

  • New private signing key. Earlier releases were signed with a key that is public in the source repo, so anyone could build a fake "update". Releases are now signed with a private key, rotated in with Android's signing lineage: installed copies update in place, and APKs signed with the old key are refused (tested on Android 15).
  • The safety PIN is stored as a salted hash, never as digits, and locks for 30 seconds after 5 wrong tries, doubling each further miss.
  • The watch needs the PIN to end an SOS. Before, "I'm safe" on the watch ended it without one; now the watch asks, and the phone checks it again before stopping anything.
  • The community map ignores junk: malformed, oversized, future or month-old items, and more than 10 reports per author. SOS pins are shared rounded to the hour, and test SOSs are no longer shared at all.
  • The watch refuses an update without a fingerprint. The website loads no third-party code and sends security headers.

Added

  • Evidence survives the app being deleted. Every recording and its fingerprint list are copied to Downloads/Security4Her evidence/, checked against their fingerprints.

Changed

  • Evidence takes less space. Video records at 700 kbps (was 1.2 Mbps) and audio at 24 kbps. After the 24-hour lock, the app keeps only the verified Downloads copy instead of two. Nothing already recorded is re-encoded, so every fingerprint still matches.
  • The map works on weak or no internet: nearby places and map tiles are kept between visits, it opens at your last known position straight away, and it no longer redraws the whole screen while you drag it.

Fixed

  • A commit had broken the build (FontWeights import in DigiLockerDemo.kt).

Watch build 12 / Phone build 17 (2026-10-02, local builds)

  • Restore automatic five-second SOS countdown on normal watch opening after setup, covering Samsung Knock Knock launches that resolve to the default launcher. Status notifications remain dashboard-only.
  • Fix the watch setup/location page briefly flashing before the saved setup state loads.
  • Add optional Appear on top setup in the phone's Fake call screen. With this user-granted access, watch requests can launch the existing call activity over other apps. Preserve the call notification as a fallback; notification-only full-screen intents normally show a banner on an unlocked phone.
  • Handle new requests delivered to an already-running fake-call activity.

This update requires both APKs and one-time phone permission setup. Phone code changes are restricted to fake calls. On-device presentation remains to be verified.

1.1.0-beta.5 (2026-10-01)

Added

  • Setup guide (opens once after sign-up; Me → Setup guide): contacts, permissions, background running, safety PIN, a test text, Pocket SOS, the watch and the extras, one page at a time, each with a live check.
  • Fix texting in one tap. Android 15 refuses SMS to apps installed from a download ("App was denied access"). The app now reinstalls itself through Android's installer as an updater would, which lifts the block; then SMS can be allowed normally. Tested on Android 15.
  • Pocket SOS screen-off test (Me → Pocket SOS): the real thing with the screen off, without an SOS, and a plain answer about what the phone felt (paused sensor, too soft, too slow).
  • Help for phones whose own Quick Tap or Back tap opens Gemini when knocked.

Changed

  • Phone app updates install through Android's installer API, so updating never brings the SMS block back.
  • Settings are folded into short rows that open one at a time.

Fixed

  • Pocket SOS went off from light taps: knocks taught gently could set it far too sensitive. Nothing is now more sensitive than Light, and taps on a phone lying still on a table need to be much harder (unless the proximity sensor says it's in a pocket or bag).
  • Pocket SOS missed knocks on phones that wake the screen on a tap, and on phones that pause ordinary sensors with the screen off.
  • The manual steps for allowing SMS were in an order Android doesn't allow.

1.1.0-beta.1 (2026-10-01)

Public beta for testing before a commercial launch.

Added

  • Beta agreement on first launch (phone and watch): testers choose which anonymous test data to share, and can change it or delete everything they sent at any time.
  • Test data, sorted into categories: crashes (with stack traces), errors, security events, false alarms, feature use, speed and device model. Numbers, places, links and names are removed on the phone first; location, contacts, messages and recordings are never collected.

Changed

1.0.0 (2026-09-30)

Added

  • Fake call (phone): a realistic incoming call from Mom, Dad or any name, now or in 10 or 30 seconds. Answering starts a call timer and a voice on the other end.
  • Siren (phone): a loud alarm that plays even on silent, with a flashing screen and a strobing torch.
  • Automatic watch updates: the watch checks, downloads and installs new versions by itself, silently, never during an SOS, and only while charging when Voice SOS or Safety Mode is on.
  • Protection survives restarts: after the watch restarts or updates, Voice SOS and Safety Mode come back on their own, or with one tap on a Tap to resume notification.
  • Voice SOS switch on the watch home screen, with live status and a way into practice.

Changed

  • Installing the watch app from the phone starts as soon as the 6-digit code is typed, and the one-time watch settings fold away once the watch is found.
  • Release builds are optimised with R8: the phone app is 11 MB (was 27 MB) and the watch app 53 MB (was 67 MB).

Fixed

  • The safety map no longer lists places from another city when nothing is nearby.
  • The watch no longer shows "Voice SOS on" after a restart when it isn't actually listening.
